# Assessment and Mastery

Richmack Georgia separates **completion, score, and mastery**. Completing a page is not automatically mastery.

## Mastery Threshold
Current target: **85%**.

## Assessment Types
### Objective
Multiple choice, matching, sequencing, map identification, and factual application.

### Quantitative
Percentages, rates, projections, capacity, freight cost, and other numeric problems.

### Constructed Response
Students may need to defend a location choice, explain cause/effect, identify missing evidence, compare infrastructure alternatives, or recommend a policy/engineering response.

## Provisional vs Final
A lesson may have an automatically scored objective result while a written response is awaiting review. That grade is **provisional** until the rubric review is complete.

## Constructed-Response Rubric
A typical 0–4 interpretation:
```text
4 — strong reasoning, relevant evidence, clear conclusion
3 — generally correct with minor gaps
2 — partial reasoning or weak evidence
1 — minimal understanding
0 — missing/off-topic/unsupported
```

## Remediation
Below-mastery performance should trigger targeted review rather than blind repetition. The student should revisit weak concepts, complete focused practice, then retry with fresh questions.

## Games as Evidence
Map Hunt is recorded as academic evidence with score, correct count, question count, duration, academic year, and skill breakdown.

## Preventing False Mastery
Avoid mastery based on repeated identical prompts, obvious labels, lucky guesses, or one recognition-only attempt. Question variety and reasoning difficulty should continue to grow.
